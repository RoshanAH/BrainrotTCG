# Short-form creative direction

Primary goal: make a real pull entertaining to watch and easy to turn into a TikTok, Instagram Reel or YouTube Short. Long-term collection/progression supports repeat sessions. Verification is a separate technical-content lane and an available credibility link, not a mandatory lecture before the joke.

"High dopamine" is a creative shorthand here, not a measured neurological claim. Treat the ideas below as hypotheses and evaluate audience behavior.

## A three-beat reveal

| Beat | Initial creative hypothesis | What to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | In the opening 1–2 seconds, show the chase target, a striking card back, a recognizable meme or a funny question | Immediate retention, clarity without explanation |
| Anticipation | A short escalating glow/frame treatment, synchronized sound cue, deliberate pause or last-card countdown | Watch-through versus abandonment from excessive waiting |
| Payoff | Reveal the actual meme and modifier clearly, then a distinct celebration or joke | Rewatches, shares, comments and visits that start playing |

A single-card clip can initially target 6–15 seconds. Test longer 15–30-second pack hunts separately. These lengths are starting hypotheses, not platform rules. Show the result; do not repeatedly bait a reveal that never happens. A neat loop can reward rewatching, but the first watch should still have a complete payoff.

## Spectacle that makes sense

- Telegraph exceptional cards through visible, distinct treatments, not only a bigger score number. Use the current rarity/finish system and actual version-specific odds.
- Give memorable pulls a short signature sequence: controlled slowdown, frame lighting, a punchy reveal and an appropriate celebration. Reuse #68 rather than open a duplicate animation task.
- Make different finish choices visually distinct. The proposed modifier-table-2 work in PR #203 is separate and not assumed deployed.
- Let ordinary cards be funny: exaggerated treatment of a familiar mundane meme, an ironic caption, or a genuine creator reaction. Do not require a statistically rare event for every usable post.
- Match visual changes to sound cues; ensure captions explain the joke when muted. Keep important text away from platform overlays, and verify current destination safe areas during implementation.
- Prefer one legible focal point over constant particles, camera motion and simultaneous overlays. A calm buildup can make the payoff stronger than uninterrupted noise.

## Live play and cinematic output

Live play needs quick manual controls, voluntary pacing and comfortable repeat use. Auto-open is excluded from the product direction. Offer cinematic playback/export for content without forcing the full ceremony on every player or locking gesture alternatives behind progression. Keep reduced-motion behavior and a non-flashing variant. Do not interpret a request for flashy content as permission to remove accessibility controls.

Clips should omit desktop UI clutter, keep the card/joke readable, and end with a short brand/play invitation. Overlay verified-source context briefly or provide it in the caption/link. The renderer must preserve the actual pack order/results or clearly distinguish presentation order from derivation. Label staged/promotional demonstrations instead of pretending they are authentic lucky pulls.

## Two content lanes

Viral lane examples: "Does the last card save this pack?", a real rare-modifier pull, a familiar meme receiving absurdly serious treatment, or a creator opening an era they grew up with. The hook must fit the real outcome; an ordinary result needs a joke rather than a false rarity claim.

Technical lane examples: "Why you can verify this pull without our server", "Why old cards still verify after an update", and "Why we chose cosmetics over pay-to-win speed". Explain one concrete choice per post, with a demonstration. The last example is a future design position, not a claim that a new economy shipped.

Use TikTok/Reels/Shorts primarily for the viral lane. Developer-oriented Shorts, deeper YouTube videos and LinkedIn posts can carry technical discussion. The proposed initial 80/20 mix is adjustable after results.

## Experiment design

Start with #77/#213's twelve reviewed posts. Change one major variable at a time when feasible: hook, reveal pause, payoff treatment, caption or duration. Use comparable source material and record context; organic distribution is noisy, so a small sample is directional evidence, not a controlled causal proof.

Track available early-retention, completion/watch-time, rewatch, share/comment and game-visit/first-pack signals. Metric definitions vary by platform; document denominators and compare within a platform. Keep a weekly decision log: repeat, shorten, improve the joke, change the reveal or stop. Avoid optimizing watch time through frustrating withheld payoffs.
